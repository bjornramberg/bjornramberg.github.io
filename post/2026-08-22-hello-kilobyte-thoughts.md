A kilobyte is 1,024 bytes. This section caps every post at 1,024 words. The units don't match exactly, of course, but the constraint is still about five minutes of reading at an average pace. While one could argue it's also stylish and keeps to the theme, it's a constraint I'm imposing on myself to stay on point.

I've spent 25 years moving between the technical side of building infrastructure and software delivery and the people side of leading the teams building it. Almost everything I've learned that's actually useful professionally sits at the seam between those two worlds: the messy space in between. Why on-call rotation is really an org-design problem, what a team's relationship with its post-mortems tells you about psychological safety, and how InnerSource can build anxiety, openness, and trust at the same time.

The problem with that seam is keeping it concrete. Give me no limit and I'll produce three thousand words (I'll use AI for that...) that say less than three hundred would have. A kilobyte is a forcing function. If an idea can't survive being trimmed down to a kilobyte, figuratively speaking, it probably isn't ready to be said yet. Or it just needs another spin in my head before it comes out.

So these will be short pieces, mostly about leadership and technology in whatever combination the week has thrown at me, or about thoughts that come flying in. Some will read like "here's a pattern I keep seeing"; others will be closer to "here's something that went sideways and what it taught me." None will pretend to be exhaustive, and all will be my own thoughts. If a topic deserves ten thousand words, it can have its own home somewhere else. This section will offer the compressed version: the TL;DR.

Thanks for reading the first 1,024(-ish) bytes. More soon.
